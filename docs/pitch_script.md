# BUBR1-RESCUE 2.0 — 3-minute pitch script

Mosaic Variegated Aneuploidy is a rare chromosome-instability disorder, and there is no established molecular treatment.

Instead of starting with a drug, BUBR1-RESCUE starts with the genotype.

In the challenge genome, we identified a known pathogenic heterozygous BUB1B truncating variant, Leu737Ter, together with a second high-quality heterozygous missense candidate, Asn1002Lys, or N1002K.

N1002K is extremely rare in population data, with no homozygotes observed in the data we reviewed, and AlphaMissense gives a pathogenic-supporting score of 0.9229. But its functional effect and its phase relative to Leu737Ter remain unresolved. So we do not claim a confirmed compound-heterozygous diagnosis.

That uncertainty becomes our scientific question.

Published studies of MVA show that some BUBR1 missense variants, including I909T and L1012P, have low protein abundance and increased turnover. Importantly, when sufficient mutant protein was restored, checkpoint function could also be restored.

We therefore ask: could N1002K be a molecularly rescuable BUBR1 allele?

Our lead repurposing hypothesis is arimoclomol. It is already FDA-approved for Niemann-Pick disease type C, in combination with miglustat, and it provides a way to test whether enhancing proteostasis can restore functional residual BUBR1.

Our second candidate is nicotinic acid. In human cells, nicotinic acid can increase NAD through NAPRT. Separately, SIRT2 uses an NAD-dependent mechanism to stabilize BUBR1. This creates an orthogonal BUBR1-stabilization hypothesis.

We also retain 4-PBA as a proteostasis comparator.

But increasing BUBR1 protein is not enough.

Our Go decision requires target engagement, increased functional full-length BUBR1, improved spindle-assembly-checkpoint function, reduced chromosome missegregation, and an acceptable safety signal.

If protein abundance increases without functional rescue, that is a No-Go.

And if N1002K proves to be an intrinsic functional defect rather than an abundance defect, the stabilization strategy stops.

That is the central innovation of BUBR1-RESCUE: we are not proposing one drug for every BUB1B mutation. We are proposing a reusable genotype-to-mechanism-to-drug framework that first asks whether a specific mutant protein is molecularly rescuable.

For an ultra-rare disease, even a negative result is useful, because it tells us which therapeutic path not to pursue.

BUBR1-RESCUE turns genomic uncertainty into a falsifiable drug-repurposing strategy.
